# Audience Profile: Solar Operations & Asset Managers

## Snapshot

**Who:** Operations managers and asset managers at solar companies — the people responsible for keeping portfolios of solar sites (commercial, C&I, or utility-scale) performing at spec. They're accountable for production numbers, not just uptime. They often oversee multiple sites at once, juggle vendor contracts, and answer to owners/investors when a portfolio underperforms.

**What they're trying to figure out:** Why sites are underproducing, whether it's worth spending money to fix it, and how to prove ROI on maintenance spend before they commit budget.

**The problem you solve:** Dirty solar panels are quietly costing them production — and by extension, revenue — often without them realizing the scale of it until someone checks the numbers.

**What you offer:** A recurring panel cleaning contract, priced per site.

**Where they hang out:** LinkedIn (industry posts, company pages, O&M professionals sharing case studies) and solar industry forums/trade publications (Solar Power World, PV Magazine, r/solar-adjacent communities).

---

## The Problem, In Real Numbers

This is the language and data that shows up again and again when operations/asset managers talk about soiling:

- "Depending on your location, dirty panels can reduce output by 10% to 30%." — [Transform Energy](https://www.transformenergy.com/blog/why-most-solar-portfolios-underperform-and-how-to-fix-it)
- "Soiling losses peak in the summer, when production is both highest in volume and value, meaning a 20% loss in July is far more expensive than a 20% loss in January." — [Transform Energy](https://www.transformenergy.com/blog/why-most-solar-portfolios-underperform-and-how-to-fix-it)
- "The IEA's Photovoltaic Power Systems Programme found that soiling causes 4–7% average annual energy loss globally — and in dust-heavy or coastal regions, individual systems lose 15–25% of generation every year." In the Atacama Desert, soiling losses have reached **up to 39% annually**. — [IEA-PVPS](https://iea-pvps.org/key-topics/soiling-losses-impact-on-the-performance-of-photovoltaic-power-plants/)
- "Cleaning panels every six months can increase output by 3-5% on average, and as much as 25% in dusty locations."
- "A successful clean on a significantly soiled array typically recovers 5–25% of lost generation."
- Google's own facilities case study: cleaning 1.5 MW of panels resulted in **energy output doubling on flat panels after the first cleaning in 15 months, and a 36% increase after a second cleaning 8 months later**.
- Customer-reported production gains after cleaning: "20% more power generated," "an immediate increase in our kilowatt production," "about 20 to 30 percent more power from my panels."

**The recurring theme:** the loss is invisible until measured. A homeowner on a solar forum reported production down **over 10% day-to-day** compared to the same period the prior year — despite the panels "appearing clean from street view." That gap between *looks fine* and *is losing money* is exactly the fear this audience lives with, scaled across dozens of sites.

## Pain Points (their words, not personas)

- **The balancing act nobody gets right:** "Operations teams face a delicate balancing act: cleaning panels too often wastes money, labor, water and equipment life; cleaning too infrequently lets energy slip away, unrecovered."
- **It started as a rounding error and became a real cost:** "What started as a simple 2% assumption during the design stage has now become one of the most significant controllable costs in the plant's operating budget."
- **Problems hide for months:** "Without clear financial analytics and active oversight, underperformance can go undetected for months or even years." Audits routinely turn up "offline equipment, missed utility credits, and unclaimed performance guarantees worth hundreds of thousands of dollars."
- **It's rarely just soiling — it's soiling plus everything else falling through the cracks:** "Common issues like inadequate monitoring, panel soiling, and slow responses from third-party managers can quietly erode performance over time."
- **Spreadsheet fatigue is real:** Asset managers "often face challenges such as poor data quality, difficulties in integrating data across various platforms, and a shortage of skilled personnel." One portfolio example: 49 personnel managing 437 vendor/supplier contacts and 10,000+ pieces of equipment across multiple monitoring tools — "operations teams often juggle spreadsheets, switch between three or more monitoring tools, or constantly react to alerts instead of preventing them."
- **Locality makes it unpredictable:** "Of two east coast sites, one stays clean with summer rains while the other gets huge local impacts due to pollen. The locality of soiling loss effects has been the biggest realization" — meaning a fixed, one-size-fits-all cleaning schedule doesn't work and managers know it.
- **Vendor trust is a live issue:** "If a bidder refuses to share raw data, glosses over failures, or can't commit to regular review calls, that's opaque reporting — a red flag you can't afford."

## Fears

- Losing revenue **silently** — production loss that doesn't show up until someone pulls the numbers, by which point months of lost generation are gone for good.
- Being the one who has to explain an underperforming site to ownership/investors when the cause turns out to be something as preventable as dirt.
- Overpaying for cleaning that wasn't needed yet (wasted budget) vs. underpaying for cleaning and leaving money on the table (wasted revenue) — the fear of getting the schedule wrong in either direction.
- Hiring a vendor who cleans on a fixed schedule regardless of site conditions, is opaque about results, or can't prove ROI with data.
- Soiling compounding with other undetected issues (offline equipment, missed credits) that quietly bleed six figures over time.

## Dreams / Goals

- Turn a "controllable cost" into a "rapid ROI" line item they can point to with hard numbers, not guesswork.
- Move from reactive ("we noticed production dropped") to proactive — treat soiling "as a predictable, manageable risk" with a schedule matched to each site's actual conditions, not a generic calendar.
- Stop reconciling data across spreadsheets and multiple monitoring tools; get a straightforward, provable before/after production number per site.
- Be able to defend maintenance spend instantly when asked "was this worth it?" — cleaning that "typically delivers a rapid ROI" is the exact phrase that keeps showing up because it's what they need to say upward.

## Phrases That Repeat (use these, don't invent new ones)

- "leaving money on the table"
- "rapid ROI"
- "controllable cost"
- "underperformance can go undetected for months or even years"
- "quietly erode performance"
- "worth it" / "totally worth it" (from cleaning customers specifically)
- "immediate increase in production/output"
- site-specific / "the locality of soiling loss... is the biggest realization"

---

## Positioning Notes for Messaging

1. **Lead with the invisibility of the loss, not the dirt itself.** The strongest emotional hook isn't "your panels are dirty" — it's "you're losing production right now and won't know until you check." That's the line that shows up in reviews and forum posts over and over.
2. **Anchor claims in per-site, seasonal numbers**, not vague percentages — this audience thinks in production data and ROI timelines (a 20% loss in July costs more than a 20% loss in January is a genuinely persuasive, data-native argument to this crowd).
3. **Address the scheduling fear directly**: don't pitch a fixed cadence. Position the recurring contract as tuned to each site's actual soiling conditions — this directly answers the "one-size-fits-all doesn't work" objection that keeps surfacing.
4. **Offer data transparency as a differentiator.** "Opaque reporting is a red flag you can't afford" — meaning before/after production data per cleaning, per site, isn't a nice-to-have, it's the trust signal that closes the deal.
5. **Channel fit:** LinkedIn posts should read like industry case studies (data-forward, ROI-framed) rather than sales copy — this audience already gets pitched by O&M software and monitoring vendors using exactly this framing.
